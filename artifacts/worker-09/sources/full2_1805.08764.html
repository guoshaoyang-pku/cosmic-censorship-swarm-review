Rough initial data and the strength of the blue-shift instability
on cosmological black holes with Λ>0\Lambda>0

Abstract

We consider the wave equation on Reissner–Nordström–de Sitter and more generally Kerr–Newman–de Sitter black hole spacetimes with Λ>0\Lambda>0. The strength of the blue-shift instability associated to the Cauchy horizon of these spacetimes has been the subject of much discussion, since—in contrast to the asymptotically flat Λ=0\Lambda=0 case—the competition with the decay associated to the region between the event and cosmological horizons is delicate, especially as the extremal limit is approached. Of particular interest is the question as to whether generic, admissible initial data posed on a Cauchy surface lead to solutions whose local (integrated) energy blows up at the Cauchy horizon, for this statement holds in the asymptotically flat case and would correspond precisely to the blow up required by Christodoulou’s formulation of strong cosmic censorship. Some recent heuristic work suggests that the answer is in general negative for solutions arising from sufficiently smooth data, i.e. there exists a certain range of black hole parameters such that for all such data, the arising solutions have finite local (integrated) energy at the Cauchy horizon. In this short note, we shall show in contrast that, by slightly relaxing the smoothness assumption on initial data, we are able to prove the analogue of the Christodoulou statement in the affirmative, i.e. we show that for generic data in our allowed class, the local energy blow-up statement indeed holds at the Cauchy horizon, for all subextremal black hole parameter ranges. We present two distinct proofs. The first is based on an explicit mode construction while the other is softer and uses only time translation invariance of appropriate scattering maps, in analogy with our previous [Time-translation invariance of scattering maps and blue-shift instabilities on Kerr black hole spacetimes, Commun. Math. Phys. 350 (2017), 985–1016]. Both proofs use statements concerning the non-triviality of transmission and reflexion, which are easy to infer by o.d.e. techniques and analyticity considerations. Our slightly enlarged class of initial data is still sufficiently regular to ensure both stability and decay properties in the region between the event and cosmological horizons as well as the boundedness and continuous extendibility beyond the Cauchy horizon. This suggests thus that it is finally this class—and not smoother data—which may provide the correct setting to formulate the genericity condition in strong cosmic censorship.

1 Introduction

Penrose was the first to remark that the Cauchy horizon of the Reissner–Nordström and Kerr black hole solutions is subject to a blue-shift instability [Pen68]. As the Cauchy horizon delimits the region of spacetime determined uniquely by initial data, this instability provided a path to a possible resolution to one of general relativity’s most puzzling paradoxes: Observers crossing the Cauchy horizon experience failure of predictability in a supposedly deterministic classical-physics theory, without however manifestly exiting the domain of validity of the classical description. The blue-shift instability associated to the Cauchy horizon eventually led to the formulation of the strong cosmic censorship conjecture [Pen74], according to which, for generic initial data for the Einstein vacuum equations

Rμ​ν​[g]=0,R_{\mu\nu}[g]=0, (1)

or more general Einstein matter systems like the Einstein–Maxwell equations, the spacetime region (ℳ,g)(\mathcal{M},g) uniquely determined by initial data (i.e. the “maximal Cauchy development”) is suitably inextendible. A similar conjecture can be made for the vacuum equations with a cosmological constant:

Rμ​ν​[g]=Λ​gμ​ν,R_{\mu\nu}[g]=\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}, (2)

which admit in the case Λ>0\Lambda>0 the so-called Kerr–de Sitter solutions, or for the more general Einstein–Maxwell equations with Λ>0\Lambda>0

Rμ​ν[g]=Λgμ​ν+2(FμλFλ​ν−14gμ​νFλ​κFλ​κ),∇νFμ​ν=0,∇[μFνλ]=0,\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}[g]=\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}+2\left(F_{\mu}^{~\lambda}F_{\lambda\nu}-\frac{1}{4}g_{\mu\nu}F_{\lambda\kappa}F^{\lambda\kappa}\right),\qquad\nabla^{\nu}F_{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad\nabla_{[\mu}F_{\nu\lambda]}=0, (3)

which admit the Reissner–Nordström–de Sitter solutions (in fact the Kerr–Newman–de Sitter solutions encompassing all of the previous), all again possessing Cauchy horizons inside of black holes. For the conjecture to be made precise, one must in particular stipulate in what sense ℳ\mathcal{M} should be inextendible. The stronger the inextendibility condition, the more definitive a resolution to the paradox the conjecture would provide. The most satisfying statement would be if the spacetime metric gg itself could be shown to be generically inextendible merely as a continuous (C0C^{0}) Lorentzian metric [Chr99a], i.e. without requiring further differentiability. This is the so-called C0C^{0}-formulation of strong cosmic censorship. This formulation would correspond to the inextendibility statement which indeed holds for Schwarzschild [Sbi18] across its r=0r=0 singularity and is related to the property that observers approaching r=0r=0 are “torn apart” by infinite tidal deformations, making the issue of their future (as classical observers) a moot point. The question of which (if any) formulation of strong cosmic censorship holds hinges in turn on the strength of the blue-shift instability.

A proxy problem for understanding the above issue for (1)(\ref{Einsteinvac}) is to consider just the linear wave equation

□g​ψ=0\Box_{g}\psi=0 (4)

on a fixed Reissner–Nordström or Kerr background. Remarkably, it has been shown that, for solutions to (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}) arising from sufficiently regular and localised initial data posed on a Cauchy hypersurface, ψ\psi remains uniformly bounded on the entire maximal Cauchy development, in particular, on the black hole interior, up to and including the Cauchy horizon, to which ψ\psi in fact extends continuously [Fra16, Fra17]. See also [Hin17]. Thus, the amplitude of ψ\psi is not affected by any blue-shift instability, which only acts on derivatives of ψ\psi. Indeed, for solutions arising from generic initial data in the above class, the derivative of ψ\psi transversal to the Cauchy horizon has been proven [LO17a, DSR17] to blow up identically along the Cauchy horizon, in fact, ψ\psi fails to be in the Sobolev space Hloc1H^{1}_{\rm loc}. This means that extensions of ψ\psi not only fail to solve (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}) classically at the Cauchy horizon but cannot there be interpreted as “finite-energy” weak solutions of (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}). (For a previous conditional instability results, see [McN78b, Daf05].) To obtain the above statement, it was essential that the natural localisation assumption on data implies that solutions generically decay only inverse polynomially on the event horizon, which is then dwarfed by the blue-shift at the Cauchy horizon, governed by an exponential growth mechanism. The proof given in our [DSR17] was quite soft, exploiting directly the translation invariance and non-trivial transmission properties of the scattering map, together with the properties of the Killing generator of the Cauchy horizon. An alternative approach has been given in [LS16], directly relating a lower bound on the event horizon to the blow up statement on the Cauchy horizon.

While Penrose’s blue-shift instability property is familiar to the wider relativity community, the question of its precise strength has remained mostly confined to more specialist literature. Indeed, the amplitude stability and continuous extendibility result for (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}) referred to above, first suggested by [McN78a] and proven finally in [Fra16], remained largely unknown—perhaps because it was originally thought that one could not extrapolate these latter stability statements to the fully nonlinear equations (1)(\ref{Einsteinvac}). Indeed, it was widely expected that the quadratic terms in (1)(\ref{Einsteinvac}) would lead to a highly non-linear behaviour once derivatives were sufficiently large, leading to a breakdown of the basic causal structure of the spacetime metric gg associated to the Cauchy horizon, forming instead a spacelike singularity beyond which the metric gg itself failed to be continuously extendible, just as in Schwarzschild. This expectation has been definitively falsified, however, in [DL17], where it has been proven, in the context of the fully nonlinear evolution under (1)(\ref{Einsteinvac}), without symmetry assumptions, that—assuming only the stability of the Kerr exterior region—then it follows that the Cauchy horizon persists as a null boundary of spacetime, and the metric indeed extends beyond continuously. (See [His81, PI89, Ori91, Daf05] for earlier work on model spherically symmetric problems and [Ori97] for an heuristic study of the problem without symmetry.) Thus, satisfying though it would have been, the C0C^{0}-formulation of strong cosmic censorship, described at the beginning of this paper, is in fact false! This motivated Christodoulou’s reformulation of strong cosmic censorship [Chr09], where “inextendibility” is stated in the class of metrics not just merely continuous but now also required to have locally square integrable Christoffel symbols. In analogy with the proxy problem (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}), this formulation corresponds precisely to blow up in Hloc1H^{1}_{\rm loc}, and represents the threshold for the standard notion of weak solution of the Einstein equations (1)(\ref{Einsteinvac}). Though this notion of inextendibility is not sufficient to ensure that classical observers are torn apart by infinite tidal deformations before exiting the domain of predictability, it still provides a definitive sense in which the classical-physics description can be said to locally break down whenever predictability fails, giving thus at least a partially satisfactory resolution to the paradox of Cauchy horizons.11 1 There of course is an even weaker formulation of strong cosmic censorship, where inextendibilty is required in the sense of a C2C^{2} Lorentzian metric. We will take the point of view here, however, that the C2C^{2}-formulation is manifestly unsatisfactory, given that strong local well posedness results have already been shown for (1)(\ref{Einsteinvac}) well below the C2C^{2} threshold, for instance at the level of data which are H2H^{2} [KRS15]. This point of view is nicely explained also in [CCD+18]. Nonetheless, the C2C^{2} formulation is still very useful to consider as a test-case for what can be proven! It remains an open problem to show that the Christodoulou formulation indeed holds, at the very least in a neighbourhood of Kerr, but there has been supporting recent work on spherically symmetric model problems [Daf05, LO17b, LO17c, VdM18] and for the vacuum without symmetry in [Luk18].

Turning to the case Λ>0\Lambda>0, already through the prism of the proxy problem (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}), the issue of the nature of the blow-up for ψ\psi on the Kerr–de Sitter and Reissner–Nordström–de Sitter spacetimes (satisfying (2)(\ref{EinsteinvacLambda}) and (3)(\ref{eq:einsteinmaxwell}) respectively) appears more delicate, and has been mired in confusion. See the discussion in [Daf14]. As opposed to the asymptotically flat spacetimes satisfying (1)(\ref{Einsteinvac}), where, for generic appropriate data, ψ\psi decays inverse polynomially on the event horizon, in the latter two Λ>0\Lambda>0 cases, ψ\psi decays exponentially [Bon08, DR07, Vas13]. On the one hand, this very fast decay is extremely fortuitous for proving non linear stability theorems. Indeed, exploiting this fast decay, the stability properties of the region between the event and cosmological horizon, under the full nonlinear evolution of (2)(\ref{EinsteinvacLambda}) and more generally (3)(\ref{eq:einsteinmaxwell}), have been inferred in the very slowly rotating case |a|≪M,Q|a|\ll M,Q in the remarkable recent [HV16, Hin18].22 2 In particular, this allows one to unconditionally apply the analogue of [DL17] in the black hole interior to definitively falsify the C0C^{0} formulation of strong cosmic censorship for (2)(\ref{EinsteinvacLambda}) or more generally (3)(\ref{eq:einsteinmaxwell}) in the Λ>0\Lambda>0 case. For work on a spherically symmetric non-linear toy model problem for understanding strong cosmic censorship with Λ>0\Lambda>0, see [CGNS17a, CGNS17b] and references therein. For the linear wave equation (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}) in the black hole interior in the Λ>0\Lambda>0 case see [HV17]. This exponential decay means in principle, however, that decay along the event horizon is in direct competition with the blue-shift associated to the Cauchy horizon generating exponential growth. The precise exponential rates now matter!

For smooth initial data, the asymptotic behaviour of solutions in the region between the event and cosmological horizons should be governed by quasinormal modes [Cha83, Dya11, War15]. Thus, the question of whether the blue-shift wins appears to be connected to determining the “spectral gap”, the infimum of the imaginary parts of the quasinormal modes. For this, one must take into account phenomena connected to the event and cosmological horizons, slowly decaying solutions corresponding to trapped null geodesics, as well as other, more subtle slowly damped modes corresponding to the near-extremal limit in the superradiant case. The relevance of these for the problem at hand has been discussed in [BMM98], [CCD+18] and [DERS18] respectively. (See also [Hod18] for the case of a spherically symmetric charged scalar field.) Remarkably, in the Kerr–de Sitter case, for all subextremal parameters, it has very recently been argued that the spectral gap is necessarily sufficiently small so as to expect the blue-shift effect to be strong enough so as for the Hloc1H^{1}_{\rm loc} blow-up result to still hold [DERS18]. In the Reissner–Nordström–de Sitter case, however, there remains a range of black hole parameters for which the prospect of a relatively large spectral gap “survives” all the above obstructions, suggesting in particular that solutions (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}) do now extend to be Hloc1H^{1}_{\rm loc} at the Cauchy horizon [CCD+18, DERS18]. The above discussion thus suggests the intriguing possibility that when Λ>0\Lambda>0, Christodoulou’s formulation of strong cosmic censorship is violated [Daf14, Rea18, CCD+18] (at least for (3)(\ref{eq:einsteinmaxwell}) if not for (2)(\ref{EinsteinvacLambda})).

The above apparent failure of even Christodoulou’s revised formulation of strong cosmic censorship (already a weakening of the original C0C^{0}-formulation!) would leave a rather discomforting situation for general relativity in the presence of a positive cosmological constant Λ>0\Lambda>0: For if Cauchy horizons generically occur at which spacetime can moreover still be interpreted as a weak solution of the Einstein equations, then it is difficult to argue decisively that the classical description has “broken down”, and thus, it would appear that the paradox persists of classical predictability failing without manifestly exiting the classical regime.

The purpose of this short note is to suggest a way out. We will prove that, at the level of the proxy problem (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}), there is indeed a way to retain the desirable generic Hloc1H^{1}_{\rm loc} blowup at the Cauchy horizon: It suffices to consider a slightly less regular, but still well-motivated, class of initial data.

To formulate our result, let ℳ~\widetilde{\mathcal{M}} denote maximally extended subextremal Reissner–Nordström–de Sitter spacetime (or more generally, Kerr–Newman–de Sitter spacetime). Let Σ~\widetilde{\Sigma} denote a complete spacelike hypersurface intersecting two cosmological horizons 𝒞+\mathcal{C}^{+} as in Figure 1. Initial data (Ψ,Ψ′)(\Psi,\Psi^{\prime}) on Σ~\widetilde{\Sigma} give rise to a solution ψ\psi on the future domain of dependence D+​(Σ~)D^{+}(\widetilde{\Sigma}), with ψ|Σ~=Ψ\psi|_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}=\Psi, nΣ~​ψ|Σ~=Ψ′n_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}\psi|_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}=\Psi^{\prime}, where nΣ~n_{\widetilde{\Sigma}} denotes the future normal to Σ~\widetilde{\Sigma}. The local energy flux of ψ\psi along Σ~\widetilde{\Sigma} is of course computable in terms of initial data (Ψ,Ψ′)(\Psi,\Psi^{\prime}), in particular ψ\psi has finite local energy flux along Σ~\widetilde{\Sigma} if (Ψ,Ψ′)∈Hloc1​(Σ~)×Lloc2​(Σ~)(\Psi,\Psi^{\prime})\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\widetilde{\Sigma})\times L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\widetilde{\Sigma}). For brevity, we will say in this case that the data (Ψ,Ψ′)(\Psi,\Psi^{\prime}) have finite local energy along Σ~\widetilde{\Sigma}.

Our main result is the following

Theorem 1.1.

Consider a subextremal Reissner–Nordström–de Sitter spacetime, or more generally, Kerr–Newman–de Sitter spacetime ℳ~\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}. For generic initial data (Ψ,Ψ′)(\Psi,\Psi^{\prime}) with finite local energy along Σ~\widetilde{\Sigma}, the resulting solution ψ\psi of (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}) in D+​(Σ~)D^{+}(\widetilde{\Sigma}) has infinite local energy along hypersurfaces intersecting transversally the Cauchy horizon 𝒞​ℋ+\mathcal{CH}^{+}, i.e. ψ\psi in particular fails to extend Hloc1H^{1}_{\rm loc} around any point of 𝒞​ℋ+\mathcal{CH}^{+}.

The genericity statement can be understood as the following “co-dimension 11 property”: For all Cauchy data (Ψ0,Ψ0′)\left(\Psi_{0},\Psi^{\prime}_{0}\right) which lead to a solution ψ0\psi_{0} of finite energy along hypersurfaces transversally intersecting the Cauchy horizon 𝒞​ℋ+\mathcal{CH}^{+}, the solution ψ\psi corresponding to the Cauchy data (Ψ0+c​Ψ1,Ψ0′+c​Ψ1′)\left(\Psi_{0}+c\Psi_{1},\Psi^{\prime}_{0}+c\Psi^{\prime}_{1}\right) has infinite energy along hypersurfaces transversally intersecting 𝒞​ℋ+\mathcal{CH}^{+} for some (Ψ1,Ψ1′)\left(\Psi_{1},\Psi_{1}^{\prime}\right) and every c∈ℝ∖{0}c\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}. By linearity, it suffices to construct a single (Ψ1,Ψ1′)\left(\Psi_{1},\Psi_{1}^{\prime}\right) in the case (Ψ0,Ψ0′)=(0,0)\left(\Psi_{0},\Psi_{0}^{\prime}\right)=(0,0). Note that this is analogous to the notion of genericity used by Christodoulou in his proof of weak cosmic censorship for the spherically symmetric Einstein-scalar-field system [Chr99b, Chr99a]. (We also observe that one can show that the initial data leading to the desired Cauchy horizon blow-up form a set of Baire second category within the class of all initial data. However, since smooth solutions are dense in H1H^{1}, in light of [Daf14, Rea18, CCD+18] referred to above, we do not expect to show that the set of Cauchy data leading to Hloc1H^{1}_{\rm loc}-blow up at 𝒞​ℋ+\mathcal{CH}^{+} is open.)

In fact, one can take generic initial data (Ψ,Ψ′)(\Psi,\Psi^{\prime}) in a slightly more regular class, i.e. one can replace the assumption that ψ\psi merely has finite local energy on Σ~\widetilde{\Sigma} (i.e. corresponding to data lying in the Sobolev space Hloc1​(Σ~)×Lloc2​(Σ~)H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\widetilde{\Sigma})\times L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\widetilde{\Sigma})), with the assumption that the data lie in Hloc1+ϵ​(Σ~)×Hlocϵ​(Σ~)H^{1+\epsilon}_{\rm loc}(\widetilde{\Sigma})\times H^{\epsilon}_{\rm loc}(\widetilde{\Sigma}), with ϵ→0\epsilon\to 0 however as extremality is approached.

Recall from the above discussion that, by linearity, to obtain the above theorem it suffices to produce a single solution ψ\psi, arising from data in the admissible space, satisfying the claimed blow up. In the Reissner–Nordström–de Sitter case, we can in fact construct a spherically symmetric such ψ\psi, or more generally, a ψ\psi whose angular frequency is supported on an arbitrary fixed spherical harmonic number ℓ\ell. This means we can replace the space Hloc1​(+ϵ)​(Σ~)×Hloc0​(+ϵ)​(Σ~)H^{1(+\epsilon)}_{\rm loc}(\widetilde{\Sigma})\times H^{0(+\epsilon)}_{\rm loc}(\widetilde{\Sigma}) in the above statement with a space (let us call it 𝒟\mathcal{D}) with arbitrary additional regularity in the angular directions. Solutions ψ\psi of (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}) arising from data in 𝒟\mathcal{D} would then share the same phenomenology of behaviour as smooth C∞C^{\infty} solutions (in particular, in view of the norms of [DR07], the analogue of the results of [Fra16] still apply). Moreover, the fact that one formulates strong cosmic censorship in terms of inextendibility with low regularity strongly suggests that one should also allow similarly low regularity initial data (cf. the role of low regularity in the genericity assumption in the proof of weak cosmic censorship under spherical symmetry [Chr99b]). One can thus argue that there is no particular reason to prefer smoother initial data, and perhaps the above class of data indeed provides a more appropriate setting in which to consider the genericity assumption of strong cosmic censorship. We shall discuss this further in Section 6.

We shall here carry out the proof of Theorem 1.1 in detail only in the Reissner–Nordström–de Sitter case, as already in this case, the Hloc1H^{1}_{\rm loc} blowup shown here is thought to fail for smooth data if the black hole parameters are suitably close to extremality [Daf14, CCD+18]. To make this paper self-contained, we will give an explict construction of the Reissner–Nordström–de Sitter metric in the relevant region ℳ\mathcal{M} (see Section 2). The theorem easily reduces to Theorem 3.1 (see Section 3), concerning only region ℳ\mathcal{M}, which is the precise formulation we shall prove. We shall in fact provide two distinct proofs. Our first proof (see Section 4) is explicit and constructs a solution suitably blowing up at the Cauchy horizon as a mode solution whose time frequency has negative imaginary part, related to the regularity at the event horizon. We note that this imaginary part may be less than the spectral gap associated to smooth data; thus one sees how the problem posed by determining the precise spectral gap is completely circumvented by passing to lower regularity (cf. Definition 3.19 given in [War15] for an HkH^{k}-quasinormal mode and also the example discussed in Section 6 of that paper). Our second proof (see Section 5) is softer, and appeals directly to the time translation invariance properties of scattering maps. In both proofs, the fundamental inequality κ−>κ+\kappa_{-}>\kappa_{+} connecting the surface gravities of the Cauchy and event horizons plays an essential role. In addition, both proofs require appealing to the nonvanishing of transmission and reflexion of suitable scattering maps for a certain open set of frequencies; this is here inferred exploiting analyticity properties. As is clear from the second proof, equation (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}) can be replaced by a wide class of translation-invariant wave-type equations, on spacetimes sharing only the basic qualitative properties of the respective horizons. We shall leave, however, such further generalisations of our result to another occasion.

2 The Reissner–Nordström–de Sitter metrics

3 Precise statement of the main theorem

In this section, we will give a precise statement of the main result of this paper as Theorem 3.1 (see Section 3.1). This formulation only refers to the region ℳ\mathcal{M} constructed explicitly in the previous section. We shall then explain (see Section 3.2) how Theorem 1.1 of Section 1 can immediately be reduced to this statement.

3.1 Statement of Theorem 3.1

Let ℳ\mathcal{M} be defined by (14). We will consider below hypersurfaces-with-boundary Σ⊂ℳ∖ℋB+\Sigma\subset\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{+}_{B} which are connected, spacelike and compact, transversally intersect 𝒞+\mathcal{C}^{+} and ℋA+\mathcal{H}^{+}_{A} and have boundary consisting of a single sphere in ℳinterior\mathcal{M}_{{\rm interior}} and a single sphere in 𝒞+\mathcal{C}^{+}. (We shall soon specialise to the case where Σ\Sigma is itself spherically symmetric.)

A solution ψ\psi to the wave equation is uniquely determined in Dℳ+​(Σ)D^{+}_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\Sigma\right), the domain of dependence of Σ\Sigma, by its corresponding Cauchy data along Σ\Sigma. In particular, we have the following well-known proposition.

Proposition 3.1.1.

Given Cauchy data (Ψ,Ψ′)∈Hs​(Σ)×Hs−1​(Σ)\left(\Psi,\Psi^{\prime}\right)\in H^{s}(\Sigma)\times H^{s-1}(\Sigma), there exists a weak solution ψ\psi to the wave equation (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}) in Dℳ+​(Σ)D^{+}_{\mathcal{M}}(\Sigma) uniquely defined by the property that (ψ,n𝒮​ψ)⊂Hlocs​(𝒮)×Hlocs−1​(𝒮)\left(\psi,n_{\mathcal{S}}\psi\right)\subset H^{s}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathcal{S})\times H^{s-1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathcal{S}) for any spacelike hypersurface 𝒮∈Dℳ+​(Σ)\mathcal{S}\in D^{+}_{\mathcal{M}}(\Sigma) and that (ψ|Σ,nΣ​ψ|Σ)=(Ψ,Ψ′)(\psi|_{\Sigma},n_{\Sigma}\psi|_{\Sigma})=(\Psi,\Psi^{\prime}). Furthermore, if Σ\Sigma is spherically symmetric and the data (Ψ,Ψ′)(\Psi,\Psi^{\prime}) are spherically symmetric, then the solution ψ\psi is also spherically symmetric.

Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1.

Fix a non-degenerate tuple (M,e,Λ)(M,e,\Lambda) and consider the corresponding Reissner–Nordström–de Sitter metric (ℳ,g)(\mathcal{M},g) and let Σ\Sigma denote a spherically symmetric hypersurface as above. Then there exists ϵ=ϵ⁡(M,e,Λ)>0\epsilon=\epsilon(M,e,\Lambda)>0 and spherically symmetric Cauchy data (Ψ,Ψ′)∈H1+ϵ​(Σ)×Hϵ​(Σ)(\Psi,\Psi^{\prime})\in H^{1+\epsilon}(\Sigma)\times H^{\epsilon}(\Sigma) such that the corresponding solution ψ\psi to the wave equation (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}) obtaining the Cauchy data satisfies

‖ψ‖H˙1​(𝒩)=∞,\|\psi\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathcal{N})}=\infty, (16)

i.e. ψ∉H1​(𝒩)\psi\not\in H^{1}(\mathcal{N}), where 𝒩\mathcal{N} is any constant U−U_{-} hypersurface emanating from a sphere in D+​(Σ)∩ℳinteriorD^{+}(\Sigma)\cap\mathcal{M}_{\rm interior} and terminating on a sphere of 𝒞​ℋA+\mathcal{CH}^{+}_{A}.

In Figure 3 we have depicted the hypersurface Σ\Sigma, its future domain of dependence D+​(Σ)D^{+}(\Sigma) and a choice of hypersurface 𝒩\mathcal{N}. The square of the homogeneous Sobolev norm H˙1​(𝒩)\dot{H}^{1}(\mathcal{N}) can be interpreted as the energy flux measured by a family of local observers on 𝒩\mathcal{N}. Note that the statement (16)(\ref{toblowup123}) is independent of the choice of induced volume form on 𝒩\mathcal{N}.

We will provide two proofs of this result. The first proof, given in Section 4, will be a direct construction based on individual mode solutions. 3.2 Reduction of Theorem 1.1 to Theorem 3.1

We now explain how Theorem 1.1 can be inferred from Theorem 3.1.

First of all, we observe that by local existence considerations similar to Proposition 3.1.1, it clearly suffices to establish Theorem 1.1 for a spherically symmetric Σ~\widetilde{\Sigma}. In the rest of this section we shall thus work with spherically symmetric hypersurfaces. Next, we note that (ℳ′,g)\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime},g\right) is isometric to (ℳ,g)\left(\mathcal{M},g\right), where we define ℳ′≐ℳstatic′∪ℋB+∪ℳinterior\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\doteq\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{{\rm static}}\cup\mathcal{H}^{+}_{B}\cup\mathcal{M}_{{\rm interior}} (see Figure 4). Next, using the finite speed of propagation, we immediately are able to obtain a solution ψ\psi arising from Cauchy data (Ψ,Ψ′)∈H1+ϵ​(Σ~)×Hϵ​(Σ~)\left(\Psi,\Psi^{\prime}\right)\in H^{1+\epsilon}(\widetilde{\Sigma})\times H^{\epsilon}(\widetilde{\Sigma}) such that ||ψ||H1​(𝒩)=||ψ||H1​(𝒩¯)=∞\left|\left|\psi\right|\right|_{H^{1}\left(\mathcal{N}\right)}=\left|\left|\psi\right|\right|_{H^{1}\left(\mathcal{\underline{N}}\right)}=\infty, where 𝒩¯\mathcal{\underline{N}} is a null hypersurface transversally intersecting 𝒞​ℋB+\mathcal{CH}^{+}_{B} and 𝒩\mathcal{N} is a null hypersurface transversally intersecting 𝒞​ℋA+\mathcal{CH}^{+}_{A}.

4 First proof of Theorem 3.1: an explicit mode-solution construction

5 Second proof of Theorem 3.1: exploiting time-translation invariance

In this section we will give our second proof of Theorem 3.1 by exploiting time translation with respect to the Killing field TT. We will in fact construct a solution to (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}) globally in the region ℳ\mathcal{M} by prescribing scattering data on (ℋB+∪ℬ+∪ℋ−)∪𝒞−(\mathcal{H}^{+}_{B}\cup\mathcal{B}_{+}\cup\mathcal{H}^{-})\cup\mathcal{C}^{-} and then obtain Theorem 3.1 by restricting the solution to 𝒟ℳ+​(Σ)\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\Sigma\right).

We start in Section 5.1 by recalling various previous results which will be useful. Next, in Section 5.2 we prove Propositon 5.2.1 which constructs a 11-parameter family of data along ℋ−\mathcal{H}^{-}, which are uniformly bounded H1+ϵ​(ℋ−)H^{1+\epsilon}(\mathcal{H}^{-}), so that the H1H^{1} norm of the corresponding solution along 𝒞​ℋB+\mathcal{CH}^{+}_{B} can be arbitrarily large. Finally, in Section 5.3 we use Proposition 5.2.1 and the uniform boundedness principle to complete the proof of Theorem 3.1.

6 Discussion

We end this paper by amplifying some of the comments already made in the introduction.

Additional angular regularity.

We have already argued why for Christodoulou’s formulation [Chr09] of strong cosmic censorship, it is clearly natural to also relax the regularity assumption on initial data. Indeed, as we have mentioned, this has a precedent in the proof of weak cosmic censorship in spherical symmetry [Chr99b], where weak irregularities allowed in the space of absolutely continuous functions were used to maximally exploit the blue-shift instability connected to naked singularities. On the other hand, one might object that for data which are only H1+ϵH^{1+\epsilon}, one cannot show that general solutions to (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}) are continuous. To overcome this objection, it suffices to replace H1+ϵH^{1+\epsilon} with a space where additional regularity is imposed in the angular directions. In fact, in the Reissner–Nordström–de Sitter case, for a spherically symmetric hypersurface Σ\Sigma, one can consider the space

𝒟k(Σ)={(Ψ,Ψ′):∀|α|≤k,ΩαΨ∈H1(Σ),ΩαΨ′∈L2(Σ)}.\mathcal{D}_{k}(\Sigma)=\{(\Psi,\Psi^{\prime}):\forall|\alpha|\leq k,\Omega^{\alpha}\Psi\in H^{1}(\Sigma),\Omega^{\alpha}\Psi^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\Sigma)\}. (45)

Here α=(α1,α2,α3)\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}) is a multi-index and Ωα\Omega^{\alpha} denotes a string Ω1α1​Ω2α2​Ω3α3\Omega_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\Omega_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\Omega_{3}^{\alpha_{3}} of angular momentum operators. (Note that the data corresponding to an H1H^{1} spherically symmetric solution ψ0\psi_{0}, and more generally, a solution ψℓ\psi_{\ell} supported on a fixed angular frequency ℓ\ell, is a fortiori contained in 𝒟k​(Σ)\mathcal{D}_{k}(\Sigma) for all kk. Thus, our Theorem 3.1 produces a solution in 𝒟k\mathcal{D}_{k} for all kk.) For sufficiently high kk, this is precisely the norm considered in [DR07] for the wave equation on Schwarzschild–de Sitter. This yields sufficient regularity and sufficiently fast decay in the region ℳstatic∪ℋA+∪𝒞+\mathcal{M}_{\rm static}\cup\mathcal{H}^{+}_{A}\cup\mathcal{C}^{+}, in particular along ℋA+\mathcal{H}^{+}_{A}, so as to be able to still apply for instance the results for (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}) on the black hole interior ℳinterior\mathcal{M}_{\rm interior} region due to [Fra16]. (Note that one does not need in fact the extra ϵ\epsilon and we have not included it in the definition (45)(\ref{anormsuchas}).) Thus, solutions ψ\psi arising from data in 𝒟k\mathcal{D}_{k} still share the same qualitative behaviour as smooth C∞C^{\infty} solutions. In particular, we can still moreover infer the continuous extendibility of ψ\psi beyond 𝒞​ℋ+\mathcal{CH}^{+}.

Generalisation to HsH^{s}.

Let us remark also that more generally, one can show for arbitrary s≥1s\geq 1, and sufficiently small ϵ>0\epsilon>0, that given generic data (Ψ,Ψ′)∈Hs⁡(+ϵ)×Hs⁡(+ϵ)−1(\Psi,\Psi^{\prime})\in H^{s(+\epsilon)}\times H^{s(+\epsilon)-1}, the corresponding solution ψ\psi of (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}) is inextendible in HlocsH^{s}_{\rm loc} at the Cauchy horizon.

Extremal black holes with Λ=0\Lambda=0.

In the case Λ=0\Lambda=0, it would be interesting to revisit the extremal Reissner–Nordström spacetime from the point of view of the present paper. Recall that, for smooth localised initial data for (4)(\ref{linearwaveequation}) on a suitable past Cauchy hypersurface Σ\Sigma crossing the event horizon ℋ+\mathcal{H}^{+} in extremal Reissner–Nordstrom, it has been proven [Gaj17a, Gaj17b, AAG18] that the resulting solutions are indeed in Hloc1H^{1}_{\rm loc} at the Cauchy horizon.44 4 This does not of course affect the validity of Christodoulou’s formulation in Λ=0\Lambda=0 since the extremal case is itself non-generic. In view of the degeneration of the surface gravity, it is not at all clear whether this failure can be circumvented by passing to a space of initial data of lower regularity.

Decay rates and strong cosmic censorship for C∞C^{\infty} data?

The above comments notwithstanding, in no way are we trying to argue that one should cease investigation of the fine dynamics of C∞C^{\infty} initial data. On the contrary! Though we hope to have given coherent arguments for allowing for a genericity assumption based on a norm such as H1+ϵH^{1+\epsilon}, (45)(\ref{anormsuchas}), or some other related modification, we do not view these arguments at present to be definitive. Unquestionably, the most satisfactory resolution of the Christodoulou formulation of strong cosmic censorship would be one independent of the precise regularity assumptions made at the level of initial data, and the cleanest way to have this would be for the result to have been true in the topology of the C∞C^{\infty} class. If Christodoulou’s formulation of strong cosmic censorship indeed fails for the Einstein–Maxwell system (3)(\ref{eq:einsteinmaxwell}) with Λ>0\Lambda>0 in the smooth topology, then a nagging dissatisfaction with the whole situation is still inevitable, despite our proposed circumvention. In any case, irrespectively of its final significance for strong cosmic censorship, the problem of understanding the fine asymptotics of solutions arising from C∞C^{\infty} initial data—and the implications of this for the sharp generic inextendibility statement at the Cauchy horizon—is certainly an extremely worthwhile open problem that very much remains to be properly understood.
